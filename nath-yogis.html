NATH YOGIS


From the 9th century onward, in the forests and mountains of medieval India, there emerged a tantric Shaivite tradition of alchemical power and darkness: the Nath Yogis, or Natha Sampradaya. Founded by Matsyendranath and Gorakshanath, these masters of Hatha Yoga and inner alchemy embody a path where the body becomes furnace, laboratory, and weapon—transmuted through extreme practices to attain immortality and supernatural powers (siddhi).


Their history is woven with legends: Gorakshanath, disciple of Matsyendranath, is said to have resurrected the dead, walked on water, transformed matter. They spread from Nepal to Rajasthan, creating lineages that directly influenced the Dasanami Naga and the Aghori. Their distinctive mark: the extreme piercing of the ears (kanphata, “split ears”) with heavy rings, a painful ritual believed to open subtle channels and confer invulnerability.


The darkness of the Nath lies in their merciless Hatha Yoga: postures held for hours until fracture, prolonged breath control (kumbhaka) to the point of fainting, ingestion of psychoactive substances (charas, datura) to transcend pain. Some practiced meditation in charnel grounds, using human skulls and bones, directly inheriting Kapalika traditions. Their alchemy (rasayana) included consumption of mercury and sulfur—toxic substances meant to burn the mortal body and reveal the divine body.


Their violence is twofold: inward (destruction of the ego through bodily suffering) and outward (warrior branches like the Naga Sadhus, ascetic armies that dominated pilgrimages by force). They rejected social norms, wandering naked or semi-naked, covered in ashes, defying castes and authorities. Accused of black magic, possession, and extreme tantric sexual practices, they inspired both fear and fascination.


Even today, the Nath Yogis persist—some in pure asceticism, others in militarized akharas. Their somber legacy permeates all tantric yoga: a path where the body must be broken, burned, transmuted to become immortal. The Nath remind us that enlightenment may demand total war against the flesh, where pain becomes the alchemical fire that forges the divine within the human.
