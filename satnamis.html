SATNAMIS


In the 17th century, on the plains of Chhattisgarh, there emerged an ascetic and revolutionary movement among the most oppressed castes: the Satnamis, founded by the mystic peasant Bir Bhan, then revitalized by Ghasidas and Balakdas. Proclaiming “Sat Nam” (the Name is Truth) as the sole divinity, they violently rejected idols, brahmins, the caste system, and orthodox Hindu rituals, advocating an egalitarian monotheism of absolute radicality.


Their history is that of an armed rebellion against the social and political order. From 1672 onward, under their guru’s leadership, the Satnamis attacked Mughal tax collectors, massacred officials, and held their ground against imperial armies for several months with fanatical fervor. Crushed in a bloodbath, they reemerged in the 18th century as a militant sect recruiting among exploited peasants, forming armed bands that plundered zamindars, burned idolatrous temples, and defied all authority deemed impure.


The darkness of the Satnamis lies in their fusion of rigorous asceticism and messianic violence. Their followers practiced total renunciation: strict vegetarianism, sexual abstinence, rejection of all possessions—but this inner discipline turned into outward war against the “lie” of traditional Hindu society. Mughal accounts describe hordes of fanatical peasants, armed with bamboos and slings, charging imperial cavalry while shouting “Sat Nam!”, preferring death to submission.


Their radical egalitarianism made them particularly dangerous in the eyes of elites: they welcomed untouchables, women, repentant Muslims, abolishing all hierarchy in the name of the True Name. This mystical utopia came at the price of merciless intolerance toward “idolaters”: destruction of temples, public executions of brahmins, autodafés of Sanskrit texts. Their guru was divinized in his lifetime, and any criticism was considered blasphemy deserving death.


Even after their gradual integration into reformed Hinduism in the 20th century, the Satnamis’ somber legacy persists: a popular movement where the quest for divine truth justified the total destruction of the existing social order. They remind us that the asceticism of the oppressed can turn into an apocalyptic revolt, where mystical purity demands the blood of the impure and the ruin of the lying world.
